If I had a rupee for every message that starts with "my lower back has been killing me", I could retire to the mountains. Back pain is the single most common reason people find their way to my classes, and the good news is that for most everyday, non-specific back pain, gentle movement is one of the best things you can do.

The [NCCIH](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/low-back-pain-and-complementary-health-approaches-what-you-need-to-know) notes that yoga is among the approaches with evidence for helping chronic low back pain. Not because of any magic posture, but because careful movement, breath and strength work together.

## First, a quick but important note

If your pain came from a fall or accident, travels down a leg with numbness or weakness, or comes with fever or bladder changes, please see a doctor before you do anything else. Yoga is a wonderful companion to good medical care, never a replacement for it.

## The three things a cranky back usually wants

**1. Movement, not stillness.** Backs stiffen when we protect them too much. Small, pain-free movements tell your nervous system that it's safe to relax.

**2. Strength around the spine.** Your deep core, glutes and back muscles are the scaffolding. When they switch off, the spine takes the load.

**3. Space at the hips.** Tight hip flexors and hamstrings (hello, desk life) tug on the pelvis and the lower back pays the price.

## Five moves I teach first

- **Cat and cow**, slow and small. Let the breath lead: inhale to gently arch, exhale to round. Ten rounds.
- **Constructive rest.** Lie on your back, knees bent, feet wide, knees resting together. Two minutes of slow breathing. It looks like nothing; it does a lot.
- **Bridge lifts.** Peel the hips up on an exhale, lower on an inhale. This wakes up the glutes that sitting puts to sleep.
- **Supported child's pose.** Knees wide, a cushion under the chest. Breathe into the back of your ribs.
- **Low lunge**, with a hand on a chair if you need it. Tuck the tailbone slightly and feel the front of the hip open.

> If a pose hurts, it's information, not a challenge. Back off, make it smaller, and try again tomorrow.

## What I usually skip early on

Deep forward folds with locked knees, big twists into end range, and anything that feels like it's "cracking" the back open. Those can come later, if at all. Early on, boring and consistent wins.

## How long until it helps?

Most people I work with feel a difference in two to three weeks of short, regular practice. Ten minutes most days beats an hour once a week, every time.
