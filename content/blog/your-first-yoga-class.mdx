"I'm not flexible enough for yoga" is the yoga equivalent of "I'm too dirty to take a shower". Flexibility is something yoga gives you, not something you need to bring.

If you've been putting off your first class, this one's for you.

## What to wear and bring

- **Comfortable clothes you can move in.** Leggings or track pants and a T-shirt that doesn't ride up when you bend forward.
- **Bare feet.** Socks slip on a mat.
- **A mat**, if you have one. A rug or carpet works for your first online class.
- **Two cushions or thick books** to use as props. Props aren't cheating; they're how we bring the floor closer to you.
- **Water**, and an empty-ish stomach. Eat lightly at least an hour or two before.

## What actually happens

Every teacher is different, but a first class with me looks roughly like this:

1. **A quick chat.** I'll ask about injuries, how your body feels, and what you'd like from yoga. Be honest; it shapes everything.
2. **Breathing and settling.** A few minutes to arrive, slow down and notice how you feel.
3. **Warm-up.** Gentle movements for the spine, shoulders and hips.
4. **The main practice.** A handful of foundational poses, taught slowly with options for every body.
5. **Relaxation.** A few minutes lying down at the end. Many people say this is their favourite part, and it's not because they're lazy.

> You don't need to look like the photos on Instagram. You need to breathe, move and pay attention. That's the whole job.

## Three things beginners worry about (and shouldn't)

**"Everyone will be better than me."** In a good class, nobody is watching you. They're busy with their own wobbles.

**"I won't know the names of the poses."** You don't need to. I'll show you and talk you through it.

**"What if something hurts?"** Then you stop and tell me. Sensation is fine; sharp pain isn't. We'll find a version that works.

## After your first class

You might feel energised, a bit sleepy, or pleasantly sore the next day. All normal. The magic happens with repetition, so try to come back within the week while it's fresh.
