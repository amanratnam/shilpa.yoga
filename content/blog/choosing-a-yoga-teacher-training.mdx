A teacher training is one of the bigger investments a yoga practitioner makes — of money, but more importantly of time and trust. And the market is crowded with options that range from genuinely transformative to barely worth the paper the certificate is printed on.

Here's how to tell them apart.

## 1. Ask how anatomy is taught

This is the fastest filter. Ask: *how many hours go to functional anatomy, and how is it integrated?*

If anatomy is a single afternoon lecture, walk away. Safe teaching depends on understanding the body, and that understanding takes real time to build.

## 2. Look at the cohort size

A training with forty people cannot mentor you. Period.

> You learn to teach by being seen teaching — and then being given honest, specific feedback.

Small cohorts mean you actually teach, you're actually watched, and you graduate with the confidence of someone who has done the thing, not just read about it.

## 3. Check what "Yoga Alliance aligned" really means

The phrase is used loosely. What you want to know is whether the *substance* holds up:

- Are the contact hours real?
- Is there a genuine practicum and assessment?
- Is philosophy taught with depth, or skimmed?

A certificate that no one stands behind is just a PDF.

## 4. Notice how they talk about the body

Listen to how the lead teacher speaks. Is it about achieving advanced postures and looking impressive — or about teaching real people, safely, for the long term?

That difference will shape the teacher you become.

## The honest summary

The best trainings are usually smaller, more demanding, and more grounded in the body than the glossiest ones. Choose the education, not the certificate. You'll be living with what you learned — and passing it on — for a long time.
